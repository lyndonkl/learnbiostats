## Misconceptions

1. **Recombination conflated with fertilization** (core `p1m1` claim). Learner's
   own words: *"recombination combines it, shuffling chromosomes between the
   mother and the dad… each parent makes two copies… I guess it's to correct for
   errors."* Reality: recombination is a **within-one-parent** meiotic shuffle of
   that parent's **own two homologs** (inherited from the two grandparents);
   combining the two parents' contributions is **fertilization**, a separate
   event; the two copies are diploidy, not error-correction. Surfaced by the
   when/whose/what probe on Q2. → claim drops to `aware`, review pulled to 3 days.

2. **Heterozygote *excess* under structure** (`p1m2`). On Q7 the learner said
   pooling subpopulations with different `p` gives *more* heterozygotes; it gives
   a **deficit** (Wahlund effect). Also momentarily labelled structure as
   "selection" (selection changes `p` over generations; structure is hidden
   subdivision in the sample now). Corrected in-session with a two-group
   numeric example (actual het 0.34 < predicted 0.42).

3. *(Resolved in-session, re-test next review.)* The "why does `p` deviate from
   0.5 / where does the `q²/2pq/p²` formula come from" confusion. Circled hard
   across several turns (genotype proportions are *derived* from the measured
   `p`; `0.25/0.50/0.25` is only the `p=0.5` point). Landed after a full unpack,
   including the persistent **base-pair vs allele** confusion (bases A/C/G/T pair
   A–T, C–G on opposite strands — *forced*; a SNP's two alleles, e.g. C and T,
   are alternatives at one position on one strand, not a base pair).
