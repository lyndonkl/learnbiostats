# p3m2 · Question Bank

Probing questions for the `biostat-assessor` to draw from. Organized by Bloom level, ascending. The assessor never reveals answers first; it asks, waits, then probes the gap. "Answer sketches" below are for the assessor's grading only — they are not read to the learner.

Anchored to the module reading: the **PLINK 1.9 / 2.0 documentation** (cog-genomics.org/plink) — `--maf`, `--geno`, `--mind`, `--hwe`, `--freq`, `--missing`, `--hardy`, `--indep-pairwise`, `--pca`, `--make-king` / `--king-cutoff` (PLINK 2.0) or `--genome` (PLINK 1.9). Core claim under test: **QC is not boilerplate — MAF, call-rate, and HWE filters change which signal survives, and the structure PCA computed here is the same correction that prevents false GWAS hits later.**

## remember (retrieval — can you recall the primitive?)
1. **Q:** What does each of these PLINK flags filter on: `--maf`, `--geno`, `--mind`, `--hwe`?
   *Answer sketch:* `--maf` = minimum minor-allele-frequency (drops variants below it). `--geno` = maximum per-*variant* missing rate (drops badly-called variants). `--mind` = maximum per-*sample* missing rate (drops badly-called samples). `--hwe` = Hardy–Weinberg exact-test p-value cutoff (drops variants deviating from HWE).
2. **Q:** Which PLINK reports would you run to *look* at the data before filtering, and what does each show?
   *Answer sketch:* `--freq` (allele-frequency spectrum), `--missing` (per-variant and per-sample missingness), `--hardy` (HWE test report per variant). These are the diagnostics that justify the thresholds you then set.
3. **Q:** What does `--pca` produce, and what is in the `.eigenvec` vs `.eigenval` files?
   *Answer sketch:* `--pca` runs PCA on the genotype matrix. `.eigenvec` = per-sample principal-component coordinates (one row per sample, the top PCs as columns). `.eigenval` = the eigenvalues (variance explained per PC). The eigenvectors become GWAS covariates.
4. **Q:** Name a PLINK command for estimating relatedness/kinship and one for LD pruning.
   *Answer sketch:* Relatedness: `--make-king` / `--king-cutoff` (PLINK 2.0, KING-robust kinship) or `--genome` IBD estimation (PLINK 1.9). LD pruning: `--indep-pairwise`. Looking for awareness of the 1.9-vs-2.0 split, not memorized parameters.
5. **Q:** What is minor allele frequency, in one sentence?
   *Answer sketch:* The frequency of the less-common of the two alleles at a biallelic marker in the sample (between 0 and 0.5). Low MAF = the minor allele is carried by very few individuals.

## understand (explain in your own words)
1. **Q:** A rare variant can carry real biology. Why is the field comfortable dropping low-MAF markers before analysis — what does an association test actually have to work with at a marker where only two individuals carry the minor allele?
   *Answer sketch:* With only a handful of minor-allele carriers there is almost no variance to correlate with the trait; any effect estimate is wildly unstable, dominated by those few individuals, and genotyping error at such markers is proportionally devastating. The MAF filter trades away rare-variant signal for statistical stability at the markers that remain. It is a power/stability decision, not a claim that rare variants are unreal.
2. **Q:** The call-rate filter and the MAF filter both delete markers. What *different kind of badness* is each one targeting?
   *Answer sketch:* Call-rate (`--geno`) targets *missingness* — markers that failed to genotype reliably across samples. MAF targets *uninformativeness* — markers that are technically fine but too invariant to estimate an effect. Different failure modes, hence not interchangeable.
3. **Q:** HWE deviation is filtered as a sign of genotyping error — but it can also be real biology. What did you *assume* about your samples when you decided an HWE-failing marker was bad?
   *Answer sketch:* You assumed a single, randomly-mating, unstructured population at equilibrium, so that deviation must be technical (genotyping/clustering error). That assumption fails under population mixture/stratification, recent selection, assortative mating, or inbreeding — in which case the HWE deviation is real signal, and filtering it discards biology. Crop inbreds especially violate it.
4. **Q:** Explain why running PCA and relatedness estimation belongs in QC, not as separate housekeeping.
   *Answer sketch:* PCA measures ancestry/population structure and relatedness measures family overlap — both are confounders for the GWAS to come. They must be *measured on the QC'd data before* association testing because the PCs become covariates and related individuals must be pruned; computing them here is producing the inputs that make the later test honest.
5. **Q:** Why should LD pruning (`--indep-pairwise`) be done *before* PCA and relatedness rather than after — or skipped?
   *Answer sketch:* PCA and kinship assume markers contribute roughly independent information. Dense LD blocks (clusters of correlated markers, e.g. around a large inversion or low-recombination region) would let a single genomic region dominate the top PCs or distort kinship. Pruning to a near-independent marker set keeps PCs reflecting genome-wide ancestry rather than one local feature.

## apply (use it on a new, concrete case)
1. **Q:** You run PLINK on raw data: 600,000 variants, 2,000 samples. After `--mind 0.1` you have 1,950 samples; after `--geno 0.05` you have 560,000 variants; after `--maf 0.01` you have 480,000; after `--hwe 1e-6` you have 478,000. Read this log: what did each step remove and which filter was most aggressive on variants?
   *Answer sketch:* `--mind` dropped 50 samples (too much missing data per sample). `--geno` dropped 40,000 variants (poorly-called). `--maf` dropped 80,000 (too rare) — the most aggressive on variants. `--hwe` dropped 2,000 (HWE deviants). Looking for correct attribution and noticing MAF removed the most.
2. **Q:** Two individuals in your dataset are full siblings. You run PCA *without* pruning either. What will the PCA tend to "see," and what does that tell you to do first?
   *Answer sketch:* PCA can be pulled by *family* structure, not just ancestry — a tight cluster of close relatives shows up as its own axis or distorts a top PC. That tells you to estimate relatedness and prune (e.g. `--king-cutoff`) before reading the PCs as ancestry, so the structure axes reflect population rather than one family.
3. **Q:** You must QC a dataset you have never seen. State a defensible *order* of filters and justify the order.
   *Answer sketch:* A defensible order: sample call-rate (`--mind`) → variant call-rate (`--geno`) → MAF (`--maf`) → HWE (`--hwe`) → LD prune (`--indep-pairwise`) → PCA (`--pca`) → relatedness prune (`--king-cutoff`/`--genome`). Justification: remove the worst samples first so per-variant stats are computed on good samples; then remove bad/uninformative variants; compute HWE on a sensible marker set; prune LD before structure so PCs/kinship are not dominated by local correlation; relatedness last so it is estimated on clean markers. Order matters because each step changes the denominators of the next.
4. **Q:** Predict what happens to the number of PCs that look like *real structure* if you change `--maf 0.01` to `--maf 0.05`, and say why.
   *Answer sketch:* Stricter MAF keeps only common markers, which tend to carry the broad ancestry signal cleanly; very rare markers add noise and can create spurious low-variance axes. So stricter MAF often *clarifies* the top structure PCs (fewer noisy axes) — but pushed too far it can also erase finer or population-specific structure carried by less-common variants. The graded point is reasoning about the tradeoff, either direction, with a why.

## analyze (take it apart; compare; find the assumption)
1. **Q:** Argue concretely how lowering the `--geno` threshold from 0.1 to 0.02 changes *which signal survives* into the GWAS — name a real consequence, not just "fewer variants."
   *Answer sketch:* Tightening `--geno` removes variants with even modest missingness. Consequence: markers in hard-to-genotype regions (repetitive, structurally variable) are stripped — and those regions may harbor real causal variation, so you can lose signal, not just noise. It also reduces the multiple-testing burden slightly. The deeper point: the threshold is a decision about which biology is *allowed to be tested*, so the filter set is part of the result.
2. **Q:** Could a marker pass MAF but fail call-rate, or pass call-rate but fail MAF? Give a concrete scenario for each.
   *Answer sketch:* Pass MAF, fail call-rate: a common variant (MAF 0.30) that sits in a region that genotypes poorly, so 20% of samples are missing — survives MAF, dies on `--geno`. Pass call-rate, fail MAF: a variant genotyped in 100% of samples but where the minor allele appears in only 3 individuals (MAF 0.001) — perfect call rate, dies on `--maf`. Demonstrates the filters are orthogonal.
3. **Q:** Your PC1 separates samples cleanly into two clusters. How do you decide whether PC1 is capturing ancestry, a batch/plate effect, or family structure — and why does the answer change what you do next?
   *Answer sketch:* Cross-tabulate PC1 against everything you know: known population labels (→ ancestry, use as covariate), sequencing batch/plate/date (→ batch effect, a technical confounder you may need to model or re-genotype), and the relatedness/kinship results (→ family structure, prune relatives). Same axis, three very different fixes: ancestry → covariate; batch → technical correction; family → pruning. Misreading it propagates straight into the GWAS.
4. **Q:** The module claims the QC PCA "is the same correction that prevents false GWAS hits." Unpack the mechanism: how does an eigenvector computed in QC turn into protection against a false positive later?
   *Answer sketch:* The top PCs quantify each sample's position along axes of ancestry. In the later association model, including those PCs as covariates lets the regression attribute trait variation that tracks ancestry to the PCs rather than to a marker that merely correlates with ancestry. So a marker that is only "significant" because its allele frequency differs across ancestry groups (which also differ in the trait) is no longer credited — the confounding path is blocked. The QC eigenvector and the GWAS covariate are literally the same numbers.

## evaluate / create (judge a tradeoff; design something)
1. **Q:** A reviewer says "QC is preprocessing; report your results, not your filters." Evaluate that stance.
   *Answer sketch:* Reject it. Each threshold (MAF, call-rate, HWE) changes which markers and samples reach the model, so the filter set determines which signal could possibly survive — it is part of the result and must be reported for reproducibility and interpretation. Two analysts with different thresholds get different "results" from the same raw data; hiding the filters hides a key analytic decision. Good answers tie this to the "filter set is part of the result" claim.
2. **Q:** Design a QC protocol for a dataset that is a *mixture of two populations plus several sib-families*, and justify each departure from a naive single-population pipeline.
   *Answer sketch:* Key departures: (a) be cautious with global `--hwe` — pooled HWE deviation is expected under mixture, so either test HWE within each population or loosen the cutoff to avoid discarding real markers; (b) definitely estimate relatedness and prune sib-pairs (`--king-cutoff`) so families do not masquerade as structure; (c) run PCA after LD pruning and check whether top PCs separate the two populations (use as covariates) vs the families (prune); (d) document MAF/call-rate thresholds as usual. Justification throughout: the single-population assumptions behind HWE and "PCA = ancestry" break under mixture and relatedness, which is exactly the setup that manufactures false GWAS hits if uncorrected.

## Misconception traps
Common wrong mental models for this module, and the question that surfaces each:
- **Trap:** "QC is mechanical cleanup with standard thresholds; the choices don't affect the science." → **surfacing question:** Two analysts run the same raw data, one with `--maf 0.01` and one with `--maf 0.05`, and get different significant hits. Whose result is 'correct,' and what does that tell you about where QC sits relative to the result?
- **Trap:** "An HWE-failing marker is always a genotyping error and should be dropped." → **surfacing question:** Your samples are inbred crop lines. Predict what HWE looks like for a perfectly-genotyped marker in highly inbred material, and decide whether `--hwe` filtering is even appropriate here.
- **Trap:** "Call-rate and MAF filters do basically the same job." → **surfacing question:** Construct one marker that passes MAF but fails call-rate, and one that does the reverse — if you can build both, what does that prove about the two filters?
- **Trap:** "PCA in QC is just a pretty plot; the real structure correction happens in GWAS." → **surfacing question:** Where do the numbers that correct your GWAS for ancestry actually get computed, and what file are they in? Trace one eigenvector from QC into the association model.
- **Trap:** "Rare variants matter most, so a MAF filter throws away the best signal." → **surfacing question:** At a marker where only 2 of 2,000 individuals carry the minor allele, write down what an association test has to estimate an effect from, and how a single genotyping error there would distort it.
- **Trap:** "Relatives don't matter as long as you correct for population PCs." → **surfacing question:** Two full sibs are left in the data. Will the broad ancestry PCs absorb their shared genetics? If not, what kind of structure slips through PCA entirely, and what should have caught it?

## Transfer prompt
One question that forces the concept into an unfamiliar context (a crop-breeding scenario).
- **Q:** A wheat breeding program gives you genotypes on 1,200 lines that are a mix of (i) several biparental families (full sibs within each), (ii) two distinct heterotic/breeding pools, and (iii) many highly inbred elite lines. They want a clean dataset for downstream association work and tell you to "just apply the standard human-GWAS QC: MAF 1%, geno 5%, HWE 1e-6, then PCA." Critique this plan filter by filter and propose what you would change. Specifically: (a) is a global `--hwe 1e-6` filter appropriate for inbred, structured material, and why; (b) what will the top PCs capture given both pools *and* sib-families present, and how do you tell ancestry from family from batch; (c) why is relatedness pruning non-negotiable here even after PC correction?
  *Answer sketch:* (a) No — global HWE is inappropriate: inbreeding drives a genome-wide deficit of heterozygotes (real, not error), and pooling two breeding pools creates a Wahlund-effect HWE deviation, so `--hwe 1e-6` on the pooled, inbred data would discard perfectly good markers en masse. Test HWE within a pool, loosen it, or skip it for inbreds. (b) With two pools plus families, top PCs will mix pool-level ancestry and family clusters; disentangle by cross-tabbing PCs against known pool labels (ancestry → covariate), family/cross IDs (family → prune), and genotyping batch/plate (batch → technical correction). (c) Sib-families share large genome segments that broad ancestry PCs do not absorb; left in, they inflate the later association scan (the seam into p3m3's mixed model), so relatedness must be estimated and pruned (`--king-cutoff`) regardless of PC correction. The transfer is recognizing that the "standard" human thresholds encode single-population, outbred, equilibrium assumptions that crop breeding material systematically violates, so the QC choices — which are part of the result — must be re-decided for this structure.
